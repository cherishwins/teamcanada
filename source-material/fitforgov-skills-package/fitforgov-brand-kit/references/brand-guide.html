§ I — Identity
01 / 09

The name carries three readings. Lean into all of them.

"Fit For Gov" is not a technology name. It's a fitness claim. The brand's job is to make that claim credible in three simultaneous registers — each of which answers an objection a Canadian CAO has already formed by the time they reach the site.

Ready
fit / adj. 1. in suitable condition
Compliant. Accessible. Audited. Procurement-ready under the provincial direct-award threshold. The paperwork is already done.
Tailored
fit / n. 2. the way a thing sits
Bespoke. Right-sized to the municipality. Not a template. Not a WordPress theme with a county crest dropped on top.
Healthy
fit / adj. 3. in good physical condition
Fast, secure, maintainable. The opposite of a bloated plug-in-ridden WordPress install with a 3.2-second load time and a 60-plugin attack surface.

The wordmark is set in the display serif — not in a sans-serif tech logo style. This is deliberate. A sans-serif mark would put Fit For Gov in the same visual family as every Ottawa consultancy that ever called itself a "digital transformation partner." The serif places us in a different lineage entirely: Harvard Business Review, The Economist, federal white papers, the kind of document a clerk archives rather than deletes.

The practice is founder-led, launched 2026, and operates from British Columbia. Every surface should make that legible — not by announcing it, but by sounding like a principal wrote it. First person where possible. Phone number in the masthead, not the footer.

If the site looks like a SaaS landing page, it has failed.
  1. Authoritative register established in Shell 1 of the Dossier framework: CAOs aged 45–65 read carefully and make decisions slowly. They are not the audience for motion graphics.
  2. Anti-WordPress positioning is load-bearing. The April 6, 2026 Essential Plugin supply-chain compromise is the immediate market opening. See Dossier №01.
§ II — The Official Seal
02 / 09

A bronzed heraldic mark, placed small and formal. A sovereign imprimatur, not a hero element.

The Fit For Gov seal is a circular bronzed medallion featuring the Peace Tower and Centre Block silhouette, with "FIT FOR GOV" arched across the upper band and "A CIVIC-TECHNOLOGY PRACTICE" arched across the lower. Two rivets at 9 and 3 o'clock. The finish is brushed bronze — metallic, weighted, governmental.

THE MARK · ON INK

The seal's native environment is dark. The brushed-bronze finish reads with maximum clarity on --ink (#141414) — every architectural detail of the Centre Block resolves, the arched typography stays crisp at any size.

Use for: social banners, dark-mode splash, RFP cover pages at large scale, formal proposal binders.

THE MARK · ON PAPER

On paper (#F5F3EE) the bronzed seal reads as a stamped imprint — like wax on parchment. This is the default treatment for letterhead, invoices, and the homepage footer. The warm palette and the metallic finish harmonize.

Use for: letterhead, footer watermark, briefing /briefing cover pages, invoice header.

When the typographic wordmark and the seal would compete at similar scale, the seal replaces the wordmark.

Three faces. A display serif, a working sans, a data monospace.

§ VI — Voice & Editorial Standards
06 / 09

If a sentence could appear in The Economist, keep it. If it could appear on a SaaS landing page, delete it.

The voice is declarative, calm, factual, numbered, and footnoted. Never exclamatory. Never promotional. The reader is a 55-year-old municipal administrator who has been pitched by thirty vendors this year — our tone signals that we know that.

Register That Works

  • declarative sentences
  • specific numbers
  • first-person singular
  • footnoted claims
  • direct procurement language
  • concrete comparisons
  • short paragraphs
  • names of actual cities
  • phone numbers
  • dollar figures
  • day-count deliverables

Register That Fails

  • revolutionary
  • cutting-edge
  • best-in-class
  • solutions
  • partners
  • stakeholders
  • leverage
  • empower
  • unlock
  • transform
  • journey
  • robust
  • seamless
  • synergy
  • ecosystem
  • world-class
The paperwork that usually takes six weeks takes one signature.

Every sentence should pass the replacement test. Can you substitute "partners" for "vendors" and lose nothing? Then it's on the banned list. Can you substitute "transformation" for "rebuild"? Same result. The brand voice is what's left when you delete every word that tries too hard.

Exception: the ledger voice. Comparison tables, specification lists, and numeric panels use JetBrains Mono and speak in fragments. "Attack surface: WordPress 60+ plugins / Fit For Gov 0." This register does not take articles or verbs. It is read, not parsed.

§ VIII — Governance
08 / 09

What ships, what doesn't, and who decides.

Brand governance is simple because the practice is founder-led. Every surface is approved by the principal before it ships. The rules below exist so that most decisions never need escalating.

Approval authority. Every public-facing Fit For Gov surface — website page, RFP response, LinkedIn post, email signature, pitch deck — is approved by Jesse James before publication. There is no committee, no brand council, no style board. One signature.

Templates over edits. Surfaces are built from the components in this document. If a design decision isn't in the system, the default answer is to remove the element rather than invent a new token. The system gets more authoritative the more restrictive it stays.

Exceptions. New tokens are added only when an entire category of output demands one (e.g., a chart-colour scale for the long-form /briefing page). Exceptions are documented here; one-off exceptions are not permitted.

Cadence. The brand kit is reviewed quarterly. The first review is scheduled for July 1, 2026. If no surface in the preceding quarter has required a new token, the review is a no-op.

CRITICAL RULE: The phone number is the interface. Every Fit For Gov surface ends with +1 250 415 5678. No surface — not a landing page, not a social post, not an OG image — is published without it. The practice does not use contact forms as a primary CTA.
