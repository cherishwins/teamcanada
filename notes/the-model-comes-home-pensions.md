# Bring the pensions home: research notes

Compiled 1 November 2026. Every fact below comes from a page or document fetched during this session. Figures are Canadian dollars unless marked.

**How the quotes were captured.** Quotes from PDFs (the CPP Investments, La Caisse, OTPP and HOOPP annual reports, BCI's fact sheet, the Australian agreement, the federal statutes, Budget 2024) were extracted as text and are verbatim. Quotes from ordinary web pages came through a fetch tool that summarises the page. They are probably exact, but check each one against the live page before it goes into print.

**Reporting dates differ.** CPP Investments, PSP and BCI close their year on 31 March. The other five close on 31 December. "Latest" therefore means 31 March 2026 for three funds and 31 December 2025 for five.

## 1. The Maple 8: size, Canadian share, infrastructure

### Membership
The usual eight are CPP Investments, La Caisse (CDPQ renamed itself "La Caisse" in 2025; its 2025 annual report uses that name), Ontario Teachers' (OTPP), PSP Investments, OMERS, BCI, AIMCo and HOOPP. The name is informal and comes from The Economist's March 2012 feature "Maple Revolutionaries" (see section 3). No official body defines the group. Some outlets also list IMCO and Vestcor among the large funds, but not as members. List confirmed: the same eight.

**How the totals were built.**
- **Assets:** the plain sum of each fund's headline net assets (AIMCo's AUM), at each fund's latest year-end: $2,647.8B. This mixes dates three months apart, mixes net with AUM, and counts non-pension money that BCI, AIMCo and La Caisse manage (insurance and government funds). Say "about $2.6 trillion", never a precise figure.
- **Canada:** stated dollar amounts where the fund gives one, otherwise the reported share times net assets. The stated amounts for OTPP, PSP and BCI are *gross*, which inflates the total a little. The result is about $725B (OMERS at 18%) or $735B (OMERS at 25%), roughly 27–28% of the summed net assets. Treat it as an order of magnitude.
- **Infrastructure:** the reported figure where one exists. CPP is 46% of Real Assets (roads, ports and airports, utilities and other infrastructure). OMERS is 22% of net assets. Total about $313B. Three of the eight figures are derived or gross, so give it as "about $300 billion".

## 3. Where the Canadian model came from

- **1990, Ontario Teachers'.** The Ontario Teachers' Pension Act was passed in 1990, after a 1987 task force report, "In Whose Interest?". The plan was given an arm's-length board. Its portfolio of non-marketable Ontario debentures (about US$15 billion) was moved into a diversified global mix, and an in-house team was built. OTPP's own report refers to the US market as a return driver "since its inception in 1990". Sources: [Keith Ambachtsheer, Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance, 8 Dec 2022](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2022/12/08/how-peter-drucker-revolutionized-canadas-public-sector-pension-system-lessons-for-americans/); [OTPP 2025 Annual Report](https://www.otpp.com/content/dam/otpp/documents/reports/2025-ar/otpp-2025-annual-report-eng.pdf).
- **1997, the CPP Investment Board Act** (S.C. 1997, c. 40, assented 18 December 1997). Section 5 sets the objects: "to invest its assets with a view to achieving a maximum rate of return, without undue risk of loss, having regard to the factors that may affect the funding of the Canada Pension Plan…". Source: [Justice Laws, CPPIB Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html). Ambachtsheer notes the 1997 Act "closely resembled" the 1990 Teachers' Act (same Harvard post).
- **CPP Investments on its own independence (Annual Report 2026):**
  - "The CPPIB Act sets no investment directions related to economic development, social objectives or political directives."
  - Amending the Act requires "agreement by the federal government plus two-thirds of the provinces representing two-thirds of the population. This is a higher requirement than the requirement for making changes to the Canadian Constitution."
  - The report also refers to "CPP Investments' investment-only mandate".
- **What the model is.** Arm's-length boards; large in-house teams paid competitively; direct ownership of private assets (OTPP taking Cadillac Fairview private is Ambachtsheer's example); global diversification. In 2017 the World Bank published *The Evolution of the Canadian Pension Model: Practical Lessons for Building World-Class Pension Organizations*. Its blog on the study (Fiona Stewart, 18 Dec 2017) says "Canada's pension funds are a leading example of long-term, impact investors", that they began with "Problems with poor governance and political interference", and that "Independent investment decision-making is essential." Source: [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/psd/delivering-secure-retirement-lessons-canada). The report itself (World Bank Open Knowledge Repository, handle 10986/28828) refused our fetch with a 403, so quote the blog, not the report.
- **The Economist, 3 March 2012, "Maple Revolutionaries":** quoted as saying the Canadian funds "won the attention of Wall Street, which considers them rivals". This is from the Harvard post above. The Economist original was not fetched.
- **Others copying it:** the World Bank study exists to export the model to emerging economies. No OECD document was fetched (see Unverified).

## 4. The domestic-investment debate, 2024–2026

- **The CEO letter was published on 6 March 2024, not in February.** 92 current and former CEOs signed. It was organised by Montréal asset manager Letko Brosseau and asked for pension rules that "encourage them to invest in Canada". It cited the share of pension assets in publicly traded domestic firms falling "from 28% in 2000 to under 4% in 2023". Source: [The Logic, 6 Mar 2024](https://thelogic.co/briefing/ask-pension-funds-to-invest-more-in-canada-business-leaders-tell-finance-ministers/).
- **Budget 2024 (16 April 2024)** created "a working group, led by Stephen Poloz (former Governor of the Bank of Canada)… to explore how to catalyze greater domestic investment opportunities for Canadian pension funds." It would "identify priority investment opportunities… that meet Canadian pension plans' fiduciary and actuarial responsibility". Focus areas were "digital infrastructure and AI investment; physical infrastructure; airport facilities; venture capital investments; building more homes, including on public lands; and, the removal of the 30 per cent rule for domestic investments." Budget 2024 also proposed letting OSFI publish large federal plans' investments "by jurisdiction and, within each jurisdiction, by asset class". It gives Canadian pension funds as holding "over $3 trillion in assets". Source: [Budget 2024, Chapter 4](https://budget.canada.ca/2024/report-rapport/chap4-en.html). **Name check:** the budget calls it simply "a working group". "Capital Formation Working Group" is not in the budget text.
- **Fall Economic Statement 2024 (Finance Canada release, 13 December 2024):**
  - "Removing the current rule limiting investments in Canadian entities by pension funds to no more than 30 per cent."
  - A fourth round of the Venture Capital Catalyst Initiative.
  - "unlocking up to $45 billion in aggregate loan and equity investments for AI data centre projects".
  - "exploring potential changes to airport authority ground leases".
  - Exploring a lower "90 per cent threshold that currently limits municipal-owned utility corporations".
  - Source: [Finance Canada, "Unlocking pension investment in Canada"](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/12/unlocking-pension-investment-in-canada.html) ([French version](https://www.canada.ca/fr/ministere-finances/nouvelles/2024/12/stimuler-les-investissements-des-fonds-de-pension-au-canada.html)).
- **The 30% rule is still in the regulation.** The federal Justice Laws consolidation of the Pension Benefits Standards Regulations, 1985, current to 31 March 2026, still reads in Schedule III, s. 11(1): "the administrator of a plan shall not, directly or indirectly, invest the moneys of the plan in the securities of a corporation to which are attached more than 30 per cent of the votes that may be cast to elect the directors of the corporation". The only exceptions are real estate, resource and investment corporations. No Canadian-entity exception appears. Source: [SOR/87-19, XML consolidation](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/XML/SOR-87-19.xml). So the promise of December 2024 was not in force on 31 March 2026. A change after that date was not checked.
- **What the Poloz group found:** we found no published report. The December 2024 package is the government's stated follow-up to his work (see Unverified).
- **The funds' position, in their words:**
  - CPP Investments, Annual Report 2026: "Canadians rightly ask how the Fund invests at home. We apply the same return-and-risk discipline in Canada as we do globally, and we invest when opportunities meet our requirements."
  - OTPP 2025 Annual Report (an executive's quote): "You work for teachers; you're a fiduciary. You must do what's best for them, not what's best for you."
  - At the September 2026 summit, PSP's Deborah Orida said its increase is "not a top-down geographic allocation decision". Reported in [Benefits and Pensions Monitor, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.benefitsandpensionsmonitor.com/news/industry-news/pension-funds-put-nearly-100-billion-back-on-home-ice/394145).
- **Canada Strong Fund (27 April 2026).** "Canada's first national sovereign wealth fund", with "an initial federal contribution of $25 billion". It is to be "a new arm's length entity reporting through the Minister of Finance and National Revenue". It will invest in "projects in clean and conventional energy, critical minerals, agriculture, and infrastructure". A retail product will let "individual Canadian investors to participate". The release does not mention pension funds. Source: [Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/27/prime-minister-carney-announces-canada-strong-fund-canadas-first).
- **OMERS (April 2026; restated 11 August 2026).** "OMERS is committed to adding at least $10 billion in new investments in Canada to its portfolio over the next five years," said Blake Hutcheson. Also: "the current environment in Canada has considerable potential". Source: [OMERS mid-year 2026](https://www.omers.com/news/omers-earns-6-9-billion-in-the-first-six-months-of-2026).

It already holds "approximately $100 billion of its gross assets, or about 30% of the total portfolio" in Canada. Jo Taylor: "We believe Canada has a strong role to play in a global investment portfolio." Source: [OTPP release (Cantech Letter newswire copy)](https://www.cantechletter.com/newswires/ontario-teachers-sets-objective-to-invest-an-additional-10-billion-in-canada-by-the-end-of-2027/).
- **Maple Fund, CPP Investments and Brookfield (15 September 2026).** $50 billion, 50-50, each committing up to $25 billion in equity over an initial five years, for critical infrastructure and strategic industries. ## 5. The case against, stated fairly

- **Diversification.** Canada is 2.9% of the MSCI ACWI (30 Sep 2026). On the reported figures the eight hold roughly 27% at home, about nine times the market weight. CPP Investments' Annual Report 2026: "To mitigate exposure of the Fund to risks related to future Canadian economic and demographic conditions, we diversify our investments across the world." The logic is that contributors' jobs and the CPP's contribution base are already Canadian.
- **Fiduciary law.**
  - Federal plans: Pension Benefits Standards Act, 1985, s. 8(4): "the administrator shall exercise the degree of care that a person of ordinary prudence would exercise in dealing with the property of another person". s. 8(4.1): invest "in a manner that a reasonable and prudent person would apply in respect of a portfolio of investments of a pension fund". Source: [PBSA, Justice Laws XML](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/XML/P-7.01.xml).
  - CPP Investments: CPPIB Act s. 5 (section 3 above).
  - Bob Baldwin, C.D. Howe Institute, 28 May 2024: "Being forced into concessional investing is an unacceptable outcome", and the budget offers no "evidence that Canadian pension funds are passing over good Canadian investment opportunities". Source: [C.D. Howe](https://cdhowe.org/publication/bob-baldwin-dont-handcuff-big-pension-plans-canada/).
- **Political direction abroad: South Korea.** Former health minister Moon Hyung-pyo and Hong Wan-sun, of the National Pension Service, were each sentenced to 2½ years. The court found they pressured the NPS to back the 2015 Samsung C&T–Cheil Industries merger, knowing the ratio was unfair to Samsung C&T shareholders, which damaged the fund. Source: [ai-CIO, 12 Jun 2017](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/former-korean-minister-found-guilty-pressuring-pension/).
- **Political direction at home: Ottawa's own words.** The December 2024 package offered incentives and rule changes, not quotas. Budget 2024 tied the working group to opportunities that "meet Canadian pension plans' fiduciary and actuarial responsibility".
- **Evidence on home bias: mixed.**
  - *Ambachtsheer, Betermier and Flynn, Global Risk Institute, 2024* (via [ai-CIO, 14 Jun 2024](https://ai-cio.com/news/home-bias-do-canadian-pensions-need-to-invest-more-in-canada)):
    - Canadian pensions cut domestic equity from 33% (2013) to 18% (2022).
    - They put about 7% of infrastructure and 7% of private equity at home, against 57% of real estate.
    - Australian funds put 46% of infrastructure at home.
    - Canadian permits take an average of 249 days, against 80 in the US.
    - Conclusion: "We caution against adopting government policies that mandate Canadian pension funds to invest domestically." The authors argue the barriers are on the supply side: scarce investable public assets and slow permits.
  - *Cumming, Johan and Monteiro, Fraser Institute, 2026* (via [Benefits and Pensions Monitor, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.benefitsandpensionsmonitor.com/investments/alternative-investments/canadian-pensions-win-at-home-in-pe-but-lose-in-public-markets/393290)):
    - $100 in the TSX Composite became $329.49 between January 2005 and November 2025, against $570.06 in the S&P 500. A Canadian-only mandate was "tantamount to imposing a 73 percent tax on pension returns".
    - At home, Canadian pensions did *better* in venture capital (average IRR 25.8% Canadian vs 4.9% foreign) and private equity (TVPI 2.1 vs 1.7).
    - The "73 percent tax" is a framing: the US result was 1.73 times the Canadian. It depends on one window and one pair of indices.
  - *CPP Investments' own regional returns:* Canada 4.5% vs US 8.7% over five years. But the Canadian slice is heavy in government bonds, so this is not a clean test.
- **Concentration at home: the strongest form of the objection.** A Canadian pension already depends on Canadian jobs. A big domestic tilt puts members' wages and pensions in the same economy.

## 6. Australia

- **Size:** total superannuation assets were A$4,767.2 billion at June 2026 (APRA, published 31 August 2026). Source: [APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/quarterly-superannuation-performance-statistics-highlights-june-2026).
- **Asset Recycling Initiative (2014–2019).**
  - 2014–15 Budget: "The Government's $5 billion investment through the Asset Recycling Initiative". "States and territories will receive 15 per cent of the price of the asset sold". "a five-year programme". Source: [Budget 2014–15](https://reference-budget.tspace.gov.au/2014-15/content/glossy/infrastructure/html/infrastructure_04.htm).
  - The National Partnership Agreement (COAG) aims at "encouraging the sale of State-owned assets to unlock funds and recycle the capital into additional infrastructure". A sale "may include… long-term leasing". The payment is "15% of the proceeds received by States from the sale of the asset(s) multiplied by the proportion of the proceeds reinvested". "No new asset divestments and infrastructure projects will be agreed after 30 June 2016", and the agreement "will expire on 30 June 2019". Source: [NPA on Asset Recycling (PDF)](https://federalfinancialrelations.gov.au/sites/federalfinancialrelations.gov.au/files/2021-01/assest_recycling_initiative_np.pdf).
- **NSW, Ausgrid (20 October 2016).** IFM Investors and AustralianSuper took "a 50.4% interest in a 99-year lease of Ausgrid", "$16.2 billion in gross proceeds to the New South Wales Government". Source: [IFM Investors](https://www.ifminvestors.com/en-gb/news--insights/media-centre/six-million-australians-to-invest-in-ausgrid-as-ifm-investors-and-australiansuper-acquire-a-majority-stake/). IFM is owned by Australian industry super funds.
- **Victoria, Port of Melbourne (31 October 2016).** Leased for "more than $9.7 billion" for "a term of 50 years" to the Lonsdale Consortium. The state said it was "still waiting for the Commonwealth to honour the additional 15 per cent". Source: [Premier of Victoria](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/port-melbourne-lease-transaction-finalised). Treasurer Tim Pallas: "We're owed $1.46 billion by the federal government". The Commonwealth offered $877 million, saying the money "was set aside for projects Victoria had flagged". Source: [SBS, 11 Oct 2016](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/canberra-owes-vic-1-46bln-pallas/gd3m7mk8v). Lesson: the 15% bonus was contested, not automatic.
- **Sydney Airport (8 March 2022).** Taken private at "an enterprise value of approximately A$32 billion" by the Sydney Aviation Alliance: IFM funds, Australian Retirement Trust, AustralianSuper and GIP, with UniSuper rolling in. "Sydney Airport will continue to be majority Australian owned." Source: [IFM Investors](https://www.ifminvestors.com/news-and-insights/media-centre/acquisition-of-sydney-airport-complete/).
- **Home share:** Australian funds put 46% of infrastructure at home vs about 7% for Canadian funds (GRI study, via ai-CIO, above).
- **The loop with Canada:** in October 2026 Australian-owned capital is buying what Canadian funds are selling. IFM is buying CPP's and OMERS's ABP stakes; Transurban is buying CPP's Sydney roads.
