On 24 January 1961, a bomber broke apart over North Carolina and dropped two hydrogen bombs.

The one whose parachute opened had four safeguards. The fall set one off. The breakup defeated two more. One switch held.

The engineer who studied it wrote: "One simple, dynamo-technology, low voltage switch stood between the United States and a major catastrophe!"

The United States published 32 accidents with its own nuclear weapons, 1950 to 1980. None made a nuclear explosion. The first on the list happened off British Columbia. Nine months later a bomb's explosive charge went off 760 metres above the St. Lawrence, and Canadians were told it was a conventional dump. They learned the truth in 2000.

Some of these weapons were never found. Part of one is still under a field in North Carolina.

Canada built the first reactor outside the United States and used it to treat cancer. It wrote the mandate for the treaty that would stop the world making bomb material. Three things it can do this year are at the end.

Every event is sourced, from the government records that publish them.
